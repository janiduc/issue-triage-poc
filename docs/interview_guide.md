# Interview guide: problem identification and requirements

Use this for a 20–30 minute semi-structured conversation with the QA lead (or equivalent).
Ask for consent to use their name; if they decline, agree on a pseudonym and record that.

## Consent and context
1. Are you comfortable with me naming you, your job title and your company in my report, or would you prefer a pseudonym?
2. What is your role, and what kind of software does your team build?
3. How many people report issues, and who are they (clients, testers, developers, support)?

## Current process
4. Walk me through what happens from the moment an issue is reported until someone starts working on it.
5. Which tool do you use to track issues, and what fields do you fill in when triaging?
6. Roughly how many new issues arrive per week?
7. On average, how many minutes do you spend reading, classifying and prioritising one issue?

## Pain points
8. What makes an issue hard or slow to triage?
9. How often do you receive duplicates of issues that were already reported or fixed?
10. Can you remember a time a serious issue was missed or prioritised too low? What happened?
11. When you see a new issue, how do you currently find out whether it was fixed before?

## People and roles
12a. Who reports issues, who decides priority, who fixes them, and who manages access to your tools?
12b. Should clients be able to see how their issue was classified, or only its status and outcome?
12c. How do you decide which developer gets an issue today?

## Requirements and constraints
12. If a tool suggested a priority and a likely fix, what would it need to show before you trusted it?
13. Would you want the final decision to stay with you, or would you accept automatic assignment?
14. Are there rules about sending client or issue data to external services?
15. What would make this worth paying for, and who in your company would approve that cost?

## Evaluation (second meeting, after the demo)
16. Triage 10 issues with the tool and 10 without; time both.
17. For each suggestion, did you accept it or change it, and why?
18. Complete the System Usability Scale questionnaire.
19. Would you use this in your daily work? What would need to change first?
